I'll be honest: a 152-page OECD report is not my usual weekend reading. 😅

But the new **OECD "Ownership and Governance of State-Owned Enterprises 2026"** covers 58 jurisdictions, including Malaysia 🇲🇾, so I wanted to understand it properly. I turned it into a simple dashboard and a Malaysia-focused infographic. 📊

The big picture surprised me:
💡 **33,834** large SOEs worldwide, holding **USD 141 trillion** in assets (about **126.0%** of global GDP)
💡 They employ around **43 million** people
💡 But they earn less: median ROE of **4.7%** vs **6.4%** for private firms

What stood out for Malaysia:
🔹 One of only **4** jurisdictions without a single state ownership policy
🔹 One of **3** that compensate public service obligations indirectly, without separate accounts
🔹 No requirement for SOEs to disclose their public policy objectives
✅ On the positive side, boards have full authority over strategy, and Malaysia now produces aggregate reporting on its SOE sector

I also mapped out a possible way forward, from a national ownership policy to cost-based PSO compensation and a central ownership coordination unit. 🧭

⚠️ Note: the Malaysia status labels and way forward are my own interpretation, not official views of the OECD or the Government of Malaysia.
